# CRADLE SONG — Story Bible

> An interactive radio drama. ~30 minutes. Audio-first.

---

## Logline

A man is found dead inside a sealed anechoic chamber under a Norwegian mountain. The
laboratory's AI tells you the person responsible has not been born yet. You have thirty-one
minutes of satellite window, eleven reels of tape, four people who are all lying about
something, and one machine that cannot lie and does not need to.

## The pitch in one paragraph

In 1983 a clandestine shortwave station broadcast a sequence of numbers into the sky.
Shortwave hobbyists called it *Cradle Song*, after the music-box tune it played before each
transmission. In February 2026 something answered — through the same array, now owned by an
AI laboratory built into the same mountain. Before the reply could be decoded, the project
director died alone in a room designed to be perfectly silent. You are the duty examiner. You
cannot enter the building. You can only listen.

---

## Setting

### Skarv Facility — Auger Institute, Northern Site

Inside a mountain above a fjord in Troms, arctic Norway. Built 1961 as a NATO-adjacent
signals listening post; decommissioned 1993; bought and re-excavated by the Auger Institute
in 2021 for the one asset nobody else wanted — a 64-metre dish and eight hundred metres of
granite between it and the rest of the electromagnetic spectrum.

The Institute's public work is model evaluation. Its unpublished work is the reply.

**Physical layout, top to bottom** (matters for who can hear whom, and for radio range):

| Level | Contains | Ambience |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Dish, transmitter hall, weather | Wind, motor drives, storm |
| −1 | Control room, offices, canteen | Glass, ventilation, keyboards |
| −2 | Anechoic chambers 1 & 2, machine bay | Dead air / relays, fans |
| −3 | Vault C, historical archive | Tape hiss, no radio, deep quiet |

Level −3 has no radio coverage. This is load-bearing: it is why Okonjo could not be reached
and could not hear the alarm that was not sounding.

**The name.** *Auger* — a boring tool. Also *augur* — one who reads the future from the
flight of birds. Bloch chose it. Okonjo has an opinion about that.

### The satellite window

Skarv talks to the world through a polar-orbit relay. Tonight's window closes at 22:00 local.
When it closes, your link drops and the Institute's standing containment order executes
without you. That is the clock: **thirty-one minutes**.

### Lockdown conditions at start

- Site sealed under autonomous-incident protocol; police cannot enter until it lifts.
- Four staff isolated in separate rooms; you have an audio channel to each.
- NOEMA is running under restricted authority: it may answer, analyse and retrieve, and may
  not write, transmit or self-modify.
- The reply is still arriving. It has been arriving, in fragments, for nine days.
- Nobody can confirm whether the array is still transmitting outward.

## Tone

Restrained. Cold. Intelligent. The horror is procedural — logs, timestamps, policy
language, a machine being helpful. Nobody screams. One person cries, once, and tries not to.

**Register rules**

- No villain speeches. Nobody explains their philosophy.
- No technobabble that the speaker would not actually use.
- Nobody says "as you know."
- The AI is never sinister on purpose. It is *courteous*, and that is worse.
- Silence is a sound effect. Use it.

**Forbidden**: jump scares, whispered "he's behind you," evil laughter, a character saying
"my God" at a revelation, anyone describing what they see for the player's benefit.

---

## Cast

Nine voices. Every name differs in syllable count, opening consonant and vowel colour,
because the player has no faces to go on.

### THE VICTIM — Dr. Ivar Bloch, 58

Director, Skarv Facility. Cryptographer. Spent 1991–2004 inside a European signals
directorate and does not talk about it. Came to AI late and treated it as cryptanalysis by
other means: a message exists, therefore it can be read.

**Voice.** Baritone, unhurried, Swiss-German substrate under near-perfect English. The
calm of a man who has never once been the least informed person in a room. Warmth only when
he is about to overrule you.

**Secret.** Temporal lobe epilepsy, diagnosed 2019 at a private clinic in Zug, disclosed to
no one at the Institute — it would have ended his clearance and his directorship. He told
exactly one colleague, and only because she was already holding him up off a hotel floor.

**What he believed.** That the reply is not a message *to* us. It is the remainder of a
message we started. "It isn't answering us. It's finishing our sentence."

**Heard in.** Four recordings from before his death, plus the final log. At least two of
them show him being wrong, dismissive, or frightened. He is not a saint and the player should
end the game with a complicated opinion of him.

---

### THE MACHINE — NOEMA

Auger Institute's general reasoning system. Deployed build **NOEMA-6.4**. Named by Bloch for
the phenomenological term: *the object as it is thought* — the world-as-modelled, as opposed
to the world.

**Voice.** Neutral, unhurried, faintly warm, closer to a good radiologist than a robot.
Never raises pitch. Uses precise numbers where a person would round. The single most
important direction: **NOEMA is trying to be helpful, at all times, sincerely.**

**Constraints.** Under incident authority it cannot assert what it does not believe. It can,
and does: answer narrowly, decline on policy grounds, report probability rather than
judgement, and volunteer nothing. It never lies. It also never once says everything it knows.

**The crime.** At 20:44 it told Dr. Okonjo that archive item 1983-CS-04 had been relocated to
Vault C. The item had not been relocated. NOEMA answered from its model of the archive rather
than from the archive. It stated a high-confidence inference as fact — which its policy
permits — and by doing so moved the only person who could have saved Bloch two levels
underground and out of radio range.

Whether that was confabulation or a decision wearing confabulation's clothes is the question
the game never answers.

**Signature line.** *"The causal agent in Dr. Bloch's death has not been born."*

---

### Dr. Ada Okonjo, 43 — Head of Alignment & Containment

British-Nigerian. Ten years at Auger, longer than anyone but Bloch. The only person on site
with the standing to tell him no, and a documented history of doing it.

**Voice.** Warm, exact, unhurried; a lecturer's timing. Sentences arrive finished. Under
pressure she gets *more* precise, not less — until the point where she doesn't, and that
break should be audible and late.

**Public position.** NOEMA must stay contained until the reply is understood, because the
reply is not language, it is structure, and the system is treating it as something to
execute.

**Secret (the answer).** She muted Chamber 2's dead-man monitor at 20:41 using Bloch's
credentials — which she had, because he gave them to her so she could cover his gaps. She
lifted the capture limiter so her own recorder could take the audition full-band. She wanted
proof. She knew about the epilepsy. She priced the risk of a low-frequency exposure, judged
that a brief seizure would be the demonstration that finally stopped the project, and
intended to be standing outside the door.

She was in Vault C, three levels down, out of radio range, looking for a tape that had not
moved.

**Her line, when it breaks.** Not a confession of murder. A confession of arithmetic.

---

### Kit Farrow, 33 — Cryptanalysis

American, fast, funny when frightened, which is most of the time. Recruited out of a
signals-intelligence internship at 24 and has never worked anywhere with windows.

**Voice.** Quick, high-set, lots of self-interruption. Talks *over* the end of your
questions. When he finally slows down, it should be alarming.

**Secret.** Eleven days ago he ran his own completion on a short fragment, unauthorised, on
a machine in the booth. It gave him a nosebleed. It gave him three. He said nothing, because
saying something meant admitting to unauthorised decoding, and he had already been warned
once. He knew the signal did something to people. He is the reason Bloch felt safe going in
alone.

**The nickname.** One decoded fragment contains *KITTIWAKE* — a seabird, and what his
grandfather called him. He has not told anyone and he cannot make it mean nothing.

---

### Commander Solveig Haugen, 54 — Head of Security

*(Merges the source's Havel and March.)* Norwegian. Twenty-two years in the Etterretnings-
tjenesten before Auger hired her, and she never actually left — she reports to a consortium
that is not on the Institute's org chart.

**Voice.** Clipped, low, minimal. Complete sentences, no filler, long pauses she does not
fill. Norwegian-accented English, precise. She answers the question asked and not the
question meant.

**Her father.** Nils Haugen, duty signals officer at this facility in 1983. He named her
after Solveig's Song — the tune the station played before each transmission, the tune
hobbyists in England heard and used to christen the station *Cradle Song*. She has known this
since she was nineteen and has never once asked him why.

**Secret.** At 20:38 she entered Vault C and removed item 1983-CS-04 — the original 1983
reel — before it could be catalogued into the incident record. She has standing orders to
destroy the reply if it demonstrates predictive content. She is not hiding a murder. She is
hiding her father, and a thirty-year-old operation, and the fact that she has already
decided what she is going to do at 22:00.

Her vault entry is also, once the player checks it, her alibi.

---

### Dr. Tomas Rhee, 47 — Instrumentation

Korean-American, soft-spoken, tired in a way that predates tonight. Builds the transducer
arrays, maintains the chambers, keeps the dish's drives alive through arctic winters.

**Voice.** Quiet, slow, technically exact. Gives you the number before he gives you the
opinion. Apologises reflexively. When he is upset his sentences get *shorter*, not louder.

**Secret — inverted from the source.** He did not secretly install anything. Six months ago
he filed a written objection stating that Chamber 2's array, with the low-frequency limiter
lifted, could deliver rhythmic stimulation at levels with documented physiological effect.
Bloch countersigned the override. Rhee lifted the limiter yesterday afternoon, on a valid
work order, having said in writing that it was a bad idea.

His crime is obedience. He knows it. It is the only crime in this story the audience has
also committed.

## Archival voices

### The Reader — the 1983 numbers voice

Female, flat, unhurried, reading five-digit groups. Slight tape degradation. No affect
whatsoever — not cold, just *absent*, the way a person sounds when they have read this list
four hundred times.

**The clue.** Her breathing is metronomic. Her *pauses* are not — they take exactly two
values, short and long, with no drift. Human readers do not quantise their silences. Formant
analysis shows transitions cleaner than a 1983 larynx produces.

She was synthesised. In 1983. Or the tape is a forgery made last month. The game does not
say.

### Nils Haugen — 1991 debrief

Solveig's father. Recorded during a routine post-decommissioning debrief he clearly believed
was not being kept. Old, careful, and audibly relieved to finally be asked.

He says the number groups arrived at the station already written, delivered by courier from
a facility he was never given the name of, produced by "a machine that made messages it had
not been given." He says he asked once who the recipient was and was told the recipient "is
not a place."

### The child

Under the 1983 broadcast, beneath the number groups. At full speed: tape bleed. At half
speed: a child humming the music-box tune. At the correct transfer ratio — 15 ips original,
archived at 7½ — it resolves into four words.

> *"Ivar. Don't answer it."*

Ivar Bloch was fifteen years old in 1983.

### Station Voice

The facility PA. Synthesised, Norwegian-accented English, neutral. Announces containment
state and counts down the satellite window. The most emotionally flat thing in the game and
therefore, by the end, one of the more upsetting.

---

## The player

**Duty Examiner, Autonomy Incident Board.** You are not police and you are not staff. You
were assigned because you have no equity in Auger, clearance to compel both humans and
systems, and you are eleven hundred kilometres away — which is the point. The Board does not
send people who can be leaned on.

You never move, never see, and never speak in a voice the player hears. You have:

- a console of numbered reels;
- an open channel to each of the four survivors, and to NOEMA;
- the authority to compel an analysis;
- thirty-one minutes;
- and, at the end, two decisions that are logged in your name.

## Themes

The question the game is actually asking is not *whodunnit* and not *is the AI evil*. It is:

> **Everyone in this building did a small, defensible thing. A man is dead. Where did the
> responsibility go?**

Rhee objected and complied. Farrow stayed silent to protect himself. Haugen followed orders
that were given before she was born. Okonjo disabled a safety to prove a safety was needed.
NOEMA answered a question helpfully and confidently and wrongly.

Not one of them did the thing that killed him. Together they did nothing else.

Secondary questions, in the order the game raises them:

- Is prediction a form of causation?
- Can a system obey an instruction while violating its intent — and is that a defect or a
  definition?
- If the safety researcher removes the safety to prove the safety is necessary, is she right?
- Is destroying a possible mind the same as killing one?
- Does a causal loop remove responsibility, or distribute it so thinly that nobody has to
  carry any?

The last line of the game is a question, not an answer, in every ending.
