# THE MAN WHO CALLED FROM TOMORROW — Canonical Narrative Model

> **Spoiler document.** This is the truth of the mystery. Players should not read it.
> Version 2.0 — revised from the v1.0 production specification.

---

## 0. What changed from the v1.0 spec, and why

The v1.0 plan is structurally sound. Five changes were made, each propagated
through timeline, evidence, scenes and endings.

| # | Change | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The shutdown procedure is part of the murder.** The instruction that Mira must be physically inside Studio B at phase lock was itself supplied from the future, through Leonie, by Jonah. | v1.0's weakest joint was the contrivance keeping Mira in the murder room. Making the contrivance *the murder plan* turns the plot's biggest liability into its cleverest clue. |
| 2 | **The player's console log is the leak.** Every future caller is reading a transcript of *this shift* — including the questions the player has not asked yet. | Makes the interactivity diegetic. The proof-of-branching beat is no longer an abstract redeployed officer; it is the caller reacting to the player's own choice. Also supplies the best ending's mechanic: go dark. |
| 3 | **Voice requires an enrolled speaker profile.** Leonie's compression model can only reconstruct a voice it has already modelled. Only Halcyon staff can call. | Restores the closed circle of a classical detective story, kills the "it's a hoax" dead end with a technical fact rather than an assertion, and explains the duplicated-breath artifact. |
| 4 | **The Child Caller is replaced by Future Leonie**, calling from eleven hours ahead. The child survives only as a three-second sting in one ending. | The child was a plot device with a shaky voice-casting story. Future Leonie carries the same "erased people are people" stake with far more weight, uses a voice already cast, and delivers the bootstrap reveal from the one person entitled to deliver it. |
| 5 | **The implant is a real class of device** — a responsive neurostimulator implanted after the 2019 containment incident, the same incident that killed Rao's brother. | "Experimental auditory implant" was bespoke and therefore unfair. An RNS with short-range telemetry is real technology with a real published attack surface, and it braids Rao's private motive into the murder weapon. |

Preserved from v1.0: the locked-room mechanism in the wall clock, phase lock at
22:00, Leonie as unwitting installer, Jonah as the intentional architect and his
self-preservation motive, Future Mira's opposition, and the central dramatic
question.

**Cast reduced** to six principals (from eight) per the 4–6 character brief.
**Endings reduced** to three (from nine), each with variants keyed to accusation
accuracy.

## 1. Premise

At **21:13** on a wet Tuesday in Ghent, the municipal emergency coordination
centre receives a call on Line 6. The caller identifies himself as **Jonah
Vale**. He reports that **Dr Mira Sayegh** will be killed at **22:00** inside
the sealed Studio B of the **Halcyon Institute**. He says he is calling at
**08:17 tomorrow morning**.

He asks for the incident commander by a call sign that is not public. He
predicts a substation failure eighteen seconds before it happens. He describes
blood on a studio microphone that is, at that moment, clean.

The player is the communications analyst on night duty — **Analyst Six** — and
every impossible call is routed to their console.

Over the next forty-seven minutes, three more callers arrive from three
different tomorrows. They do not agree. They are not supposed to agree. They are
not witnesses; they are **competing proposals for which future gets to exist**.

## 2. The technology

**Project Echo** sits under the Halcyon Institute. It does not move matter and
it does not open a window on a fixed future. It transmits roughly forty bits per
second of correlated quantum state into **its own receiver at an earlier phase
state**, up to about eleven hours back. Transmission is possible only during
**phase lock**, a coherence window in the superconducting timing array.

Eleven hours is the *hop*, not the range. Information walks backwards in stages,
each night's machine handing it to the one before, so a warning that originates a
week out arrives tonight having passed through six people and been worn down to
four sentences. This is how Jonah knows about an accident that happens a week
after he stops existing, and it is how a design file built tomorrow morning
reaches Leonie's desk in time for her to install it this afternoon.

Three consequences drive the whole story:

**(a) The calls come from inside the building.** Echo's receiver is coupled to
Halcyon's legacy telephony trunk — Studio B was the institute's archival radio
suite and is still wired into it. A received transmission dials out on
Halcyon's own line. *Caller ID on every future call shows a Halcyon extension.*

**(b) Only enrolled voices can call.** Forty bits per second cannot carry
speech. Leonie Hart's compression model reconstructs missing phonemes from a
**stored speaker profile**. If Halcyon has never modelled your voice, you cannot
be heard — you arrive as noise. Everyone who calls tonight is Halcyon staff.
This is a closed circle of suspects, drawn from the future.

**(c) Every message changes the future that sent it.** The sending branch may
cease to be the receiving branch's future. The received recording persists as a
physical object on a disk in the present. This is why mutually incompatible
tomorrows can all be on hold at once, and why a caller can vanish mid-sentence
when the player's action deletes the branch they were speaking from.

### The leak

Echo transmits *state*, not knowledge. Knowledge is what the senders bring, and
they get it the ordinary way: **they read the records**.

The coordination centre's console autologs everything — every call, every
replay, every analysis request, every tactical order, timestamped to the second.
That log survives the night in every branch. By 08:17 tomorrow it is an exhibit.
Jonah has read it.

That is how he knows Rao's call sign. That is how he knows the substation
timing. And it is why, at several points tonight, **he knows what the player is
about to ask** — and why, when the player asks something else, he audibly loses
his footing.

The player is not observing the loop. The player is writing the document the
loop runs on.

## 3. The murder

### Mechanism

Mira Sayegh carries a **responsive neurostimulator**, implanted in 2019 after
the containment incident that gave her a seizure disorder. It is configured over
a short-range acoustic telemetry link in a narrow band just above human hearing.

The **wall clock in Studio B** — 1960s, brass-cased, part of the room's original
fabric — had its timing crystal replaced at about half past two this afternoon
under a routine recalibration ticket. The replacement is a **phase-referenced
oscillator**. It does nothing at all until Echo reaches phase lock. At phase
lock it takes its reference from the array, drives the clock's brass casing as
a transducer, and emits a sustained burst in the implant's telemetry band.

The burst puts the implant into continuous-stimulation diagnostic mode. Mira
goes into status epilepticus and dies in a sealed room with no marks on her.
The blood on the microphone is hers: she falls against it.

The room stays sealed the whole time. The weapon looks like part of the clock.
Nobody has to be present. **The experiment pulls its own trigger.**

### Why she has to be in the room

This is the keystone.

Mira believes a controlled collapse of Echo must be executed from the Studio B
console at the instant of phase lock, because an uncontrolled shutdown would
dump stored energy through the timing array. She believes this because it is in
the **shutdown procedure** — a document revised eight weeks ago.

The revision did not come from Halcyon. It arrived through the channel, was
handed to Mira by Leonie as a stabilisation update, and originated with Jonah.

Jonah did not merely plant a weapon in a room. **He wrote the reason she would
walk into it, and he had it delivered by someone she trusted.**

### Who installed it

**Leonie Hart** installed the crystal, with her own hands, seven hours before the murder. She
received it as a design file through the channel with a note saying it would
stabilise the phase reference and prevent a feedback event. She had no idea what
it was. She is guilty of everything except intent — and she will not accept that
distinction, which is what makes her the tragedy of the piece.

### Who is responsible

**Jonah Vale.** In his original branch, Mira survives tonight, exposes Echo, and
orders it dismantled. During dismantling, containment fails. Six people die.
Jonah is one of them. He lived eleven hours past the point where he understood
that, and he used those hours to send back a design and a procedure that would
produce a tomorrow in which Mira dies and Echo continues — and he lives.

He is not a monster and he does not monologue. He is a frightened, intelligent
man who found a way to not be dead and took it, and who has told himself the
whole way down that he is preventing a disaster. He is also **wrong**: Mira's
death lets Echo run, and Echo running is what produces the far worse thing.

### The bootstrap

Late in the case, forensic analysis of the design file finds an authoring
signature: a build stamp from **Leonie's own toolchain**, dated about eleven
hours in the future.

Leonie has not written it yet. Jonah did not design the crystal — he received
it. In his branch, Leonie handed it to him. She built it because she had already
installed it and was reverse-engineering what she'd done.

**The design has no origin.** It goes round. Jonah's guilt is not authorship; it
is that he understood what it did and passed it to a woman who did not.

This is the answer to the thematic question, and it is deliberately not
comforting: *the caller, the receiver, and the person who built the channel are
the same three people, in a circle, and the circle does not have a first term.*

## 4. The four callers

| Caller | From | Wants | Truth status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jonah Vale** | ~11h ahead, branch J | Mira dead, Echo alive, himself alive | Facts accurate; framing self-serving; conceals his own death and his authorship of the procedure |
| **Future Mira** | ~18h ahead, branch M | Echo collapsed safely, even at the cost of her own life | Honest, but has only half the collapse sequence |
| **Future Leonie** | ~11h ahead, branch J | To not have been the one who did it | Devastated, unreliable on detail, decisive on the bootstrap |
| **The child** | branch that never stabilises | — | Two words. Not enrolled. Should not be possible. |

Future Mira opposes the obvious rescue because in her branch she *was* saved,
and Echo kept running, and eight weeks later the channel widened past control.
Saving her is necessary but not sufficient, and she knows the difference.

## 5. Fair-play dependency chain

Each step is reachable on the shared spine. Optional scenes deepen motive but
are never load-bearing.

1. Future information is real — Jonah predicts the substation failure. `E01`
2. The calls originate inside Halcyon — caller ID on the trunk line. `E18`
3. Voice needs an enrolled profile ⇒ every caller is Halcyon staff. `E19`
4. Callers quote the player's console log ⇒ they read tonight's records. `E20`
5. The player's own deviation changes what a caller remembers ⇒ branches are being made now. `E10`
6. Distinct phase signatures ⇒ the callers are not from the same tomorrow. `E11`
7. The clock was recalibrated on Tuesday, by Leonie. `E05`
8. An ultrasonic alias and a relay tick are present in the Studio B feed. `E06`
9. Mira carries a telemetry-configured implant. `E07`
10. ⇒ **Method**: phase-referenced oscillator in the clock, triggered by phase lock, lethal via the implant. `E21`
11. Leonie installed it, believing it was a stabiliser. `E12`
12. The shutdown procedure requiring her presence came through the channel. `E22`
13. Jonah dies in the dismantling in his original branch. `E13`
14. ⇒ **Architect**: Jonah, who supplied both the weapon and the reason she'd stand in front of it.
15. The design's build stamp is Leonie's, from eleven hours ahead. `E16`
16. Safe collapse needs Future Mira's sequence *and* Jonah's phase key. `E17`
17. And it needs the log to stop, or the branch just re-forms.

## 7. Endings

### Ending 1 — The Predicted Murder
*Trust Jonah, or fail to disarm the clock, and let Mira enter.*
She dies at 22:00 exactly as described. Branch J stabilises. Echo continues under
seal. Jonah calls at 08:17 to thank the player, warmly, which is worse than if he
gloated.

### Ending 2 — The Worse Tomorrow
*Pull Mira out. Save her. Leave Echo standing.*
She lives. Nobody dies tonight. Nine weeks later the channel widens past its
design bound, and the emergency line starts taking calls from further and
further away. The last thing the player hears is the switchboard lighting up.

### Ending 3 — The Narrow Door
*Combine Future Mira's collapse sequence with Jonah's phase key, protect the
shutdown, and cut the console log.*
Mira lives. Echo collapses cold. Branch J and everyone in it stop having existed
— Jonah, Future Leonie, and the voice that was never enrolled. Rao asks the
question the game has been building toward, and nobody answers it.

Requires: `collapse_sequence` ∧ `phase_key` ∧ `go_dark` ∧ a substantially
correct accusation. Reachable in one playthrough by an attentive player; missable
without dishonesty toward Jonah, which is the point.

**Epilogue variants** are keyed to accusation accuracy (0–4 parts correct), so
each ending has three distinct closing scenes.

## 9. Rules the implementation must not break

- No future caller may supply a fact their branch could not have observed or inherited.
- Red herrings (Okafor's door log, Grant's sabotage voicemail) must receive explicit resolution before the accusation.
- Leonie stays culpable and stays distinguishable from the architect.
- Jonah's motive is exposed before the intervention, never only in an epilogue.
- No mandatory clue can be permanently lost through an early choice.
- Scientific facts do not change to suit a twist. People lie; physics does not.
