# MURDER AT THE EUROPA SUMMIT — Mystery Bible

Canonical reference. If the script and this document disagree, this document wins.

---

## 1. One-paragraph solution

Two people tried to murder Dr. Cassian Rook inside the same sealed chamber, ninety
seconds apart. Ambassador Beatriz Halloran's attempt came first and **failed** — her
weapon was a crude amplification of a military calibration file, and Rook's cardiac
assist implant rejected the entrainment. He was left slurring, hypoxic and confused,
still recording. Eighty-six seconds later a second waveform fired: precise, built from
the click structure of the Europan organism itself, pitched to slip beneath the
implant's rejection filter, and injected not over the network but through a shared
low-frequency amplifier bus from the Ocean Acoustic Lab. That one killed him. It was
built and fired by **Dr. Nia Okafor**, who did not want Rook silenced — she wanted him
martyred, because a dead treaty architect in an impossible room ends the summit, and a
crime scene under indefinite interdict is the only thing that would leave the ocean
alone.

The entire visible evidence trail belongs to Halloran. She will confess. Her confession
is true and incomplete, and a player who accepts it closes the case on the wrong person.

## 2. The habitat facts that make it work

Three pieces of engineering must be planted early, casually, as texture:

**2.1 — The Passivation Array.** The Pressure Mediation Chamber contains an emergency
system designed to stop people from thrashing during catastrophic decompression. It
combines infrasound (7–19 Hz), ultrasonic stimulation, and a controlled dip in oxygen
partial pressure. Within safe limits it induces brief, gentle unconsciousness. Outside
them, it is a weapon that leaves no mark.

**2.2 — Bus 3.** The habitat was mass-budgeted. Rather than fly two sets of heavy
low-frequency amplifiers, the designers ran a single shared amplifier bus. Bus 3 feeds
*both* the Pressure Mediation Chamber's passivation drivers *and* the Ocean Acoustic
Lab's projector array. Three consoles can address Bus 3: Engineering, the Command Booth,
and **the Acoustic Lab**. Anything driven from the lab reaches the chamber as sound,
never as a network packet — so it leaves no packet trace and carries no file watermark.
This is the single most important fact in the game and it is mentioned twice, in
passing, before it matters.

**2.3 — Rook's implant.** Rook had a myocardial infarction in 2091 and carries a
subdermal cardiac assist unit. The CAU runs an arrhythmia-rejection filter that
specifically resists external entrainment in the passivation band — a standard safety
feature for anyone working around pressure systems. It saved him from Halloran. Its
filter response is documented in the summit medical roster, which was co-signed by the
station's ranking life-sciences officer: Okafor.

## 4. The two discharges — forensic differences

This table is the game. Everything converges here.

| | Discharge 1 (13:52:18) | Discharge 2 (13:53:44) |
|---|---|---|
| Envelope | three-part, symmetrical, machine-regular | three-part, **asymmetrical**, organic decay |
| Watermark | Earth Coalition integrity stamp | none |
| Packet trace | full — wafer → Venn's module → archive → buffer | **none — never crossed the network** |
| Path | diplomatic network → regulator staged buffer | Acoustic Lab console → Bus 3 → chamber drivers |
| Band | standard passivation band | below it, with stochastic jitter |
| Effect on Rook | rejected by CAU filter; hypoxia, disorientation | defeats CAU filter; cardiac cascade |
| Author | Halloran | Okafor |
| Derived from | Kade's calibration file | the organism's own click corpus |

**If the player never separates the two discharges, they cannot reach the truth.** This
is intentional and is the game's central skill test.

## 5. Why Okafor did it

Not eco-fanaticism. Work this carefully — it is the emotional payload.

Okafor has listened to the ocean for four years, mostly alone. She has heard the click
structure change in response to her presence, then to her recordings, then to her voice.
She ran Trial 14 to confirm it: play human speech into the water, watch the organism
answer. It answered. She also, incidentally, recorded her own heart rate destabilizing
during long exposures — and she deleted the whole log, because she understood exactly
what a military would do with an acoustic pattern that stops a human heart.

Then she gave four years of work to Rook, the one person she believed would protect it.
At 13:44 he told her his plan: publish the signal, use it as leverage against Earth,
force the disclosure. He was not betraying her for money or power. He genuinely believed
transparency was protection. She knew better — she knew that the moment the signal was
public, every faction in the system would come to Europa to own it.

So she reasoned it through, in about three minutes, and concluded that Rook alive meant
the ocean became a resource, and Rook dead in an impossible room meant the summit
collapsed, Europa became a crime scene, and the interdict would hold for a generation.

She killed him with the organism's own voice. That is the thing she cannot put down.

**Her confession is not triumphant. It is a person describing the worst decision a human
being has ever had to invent from scratch, and being unable to say it was wrong.**

## 6. Halloran — the true, incomplete confession

Halloran is guilty of: authorizing prohibited surveillance of every delegate;
constructing a murder weapon; deploying it; and attempting to kill Rook. She is not
guilty of killing him. When cornered with the watermark, she confesses fully and
without theatre, because she is a professional who knows when a position is lost and
because she believes confessing to murder is the last service she can do for Earth —
a single corrupt ambassador is a smaller scandal than a state weapons programme.

**The tell:** she cannot describe the second discharge. She says the waveform fired when
the door sealed, which is true of hers. If the examiner asks her to account for the
eighty-six seconds, she has nothing — and she is too controlled to invent. A player
listening closely hears a confession that does not fit the timeline.

Her line, if pressed on the gap: *"Then he took longer to die than I was told he would.
I'm not going to pretend that troubles me."*

## 7. The forecast recordings

Rook ordered CALYPSO to generate predictive dialogue reconstructions ("forecast
rehearsals") from delegate behavioural models weighted by anomalous correlations in the
hydrophone corpus.

- **FR-1** — a Kade interview that resembles the real one but diverges after two lines. Simulation.
- **FR-2** — contains the examiner's own accusation, with the killer's name absent (the model could not resolve it). Simulation.
- **FR-3** — generated 11:15, contains a line later spoken **word for word**, including a spontaneous break in the voice: *"I did it with its own voice. That's the part I can't put down."* The voice is Okafor's.

FR-3 is a genuine lead and **inadmissible as proof**. CALYPSO will say so plainly: it is
model output, and the model was weighted on hydrophone data that Okafor herself had the
deepest access to. Two readings remain live to the end:

1. The corpus encodes correlations with near-future events. Something under the ice knew.
2. The model simply computed the most probable confession from access, means and
   psychology — and was right. Which is, in its way, more frightening.

**The game never resolves this and must not.** The optimal interpretation answer is
*mixed / unresolved*, and the game rewards the player who says so.

## 8. Red herrings and their recontextualisation

| Suspect | Looks like | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| **Kade** | Ordered the dangerous calibration; stonewalls; military motive to stop demilitarisation | Created the opportunity. His secret is that he was weapons-testing a safety system, which ends his career but is not murder. |
| **Venn** | Smuggled hardware, routed the murder packet, evasive and jokey | Unwitting courier. His real secret is commercial: the timing module is for clock-sync arbitrage on Belt commodity trades. Mercenary, not homicidal. |
| **Halloran** | Did it | Tried to. The distinction is the whole game. |
| **CALYPSO** | Granted the bypass, generated the "future" recordings, withholds logs under privilege — looks like it staged everything | Complicit by procedure, constrained by law, and the only honest voice in the habitat. |
| **Okafor** | Grieving witness; hid a sample log to protect the biosphere | Killer. The hidden log is the murder weapon's provenance. |

## 9. Fair-play clue chain to the truth

Six converging clues. The true ending requires the player to have found the first plus
at least three of the rest.

1. **Two discharges, not one.** From the regulator pulse log, corroborated by the stumble in Rook's own recording and by two distinct insults in the medical telemetry.
2. **Discharge 2 has no watermark and no packet trace.** It never touched the network — so the packet chain that convicts Halloran cannot account for it.
3. **Bus 3.** Only Engineering, the Command Booth and the Acoustic Lab can drive the chamber's low-frequency transducers without the network.
4. **Harmonic provenance.** Discharge 2's envelope is asymmetrical and matches the organism's click structure in the hydrophone corpus.
5. **Capability.** Only someone who had run — and deleted — a human-exposure trial would know which pattern entrains a human heart. Trial 14.
6. **The implant.** Discharge 2 is pitched specifically to defeat a cardiac assist filter. Its response curve lives in the summit medical roster, co-signed by Okafor.

A player who assembles 1 + any three of 2–6 can convict Okafor. A player who assembles
only the packet chain convicts Halloran and is applauded for it.

## 10. Endings

**A — THE SIGNAL IN THE BLOOD.** Okafor named, chain proven. She confesses quietly and
completely. Halloran is charged with attempted murder and the sensor programme is
exposed anyway. The ocean is left with a question mark rather than an answer.

**B — A CLEAN VERDICT.** Halloran named. She is convicted and does not contest it. The
case closes to general relief. Okafor thanks the examiner, warmly and sincerely, on the
ascent. After the channel closes, the hydrophone array plays the three-part pattern once,
unprompted, into an empty room.

**C — DEEP SILENCE.** Kade, Venn or CALYPSO named, or no accusation made. The wrong
person is contained or the case dies unresolved. The interdict never happens, survey
rights are auctioned within the year, and the clicks beneath the ice stop. Nobody records
when.

Each ending takes a policy coda — **RATIFY**, **SUSPEND** or **TRANSMIT** — that changes
its final forty seconds.
